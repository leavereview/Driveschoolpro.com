**For private practice a learner must be insured on the car they are practising in. Your instructor policy doesn't do that for them.** GOV.UK gives two routes when the car belongs to someone else: the owner's policy covers the pupil as a learner driver, or the pupil takes out their own learner driver policy on that car. If the pupil owns the car, they need their own insurance as a learner.

Pupils and parents ask their instructor about this before they ask anyone else. This guide sets out what the rules say, the questions a pupil should put to an insurer, and what changes on test day and after a pass. It sits alongside our guide to [driving school insurance](/blog/driving-school-insurance-guide/), which covers the cover you need for your own tuition car.

The rules here come from [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/driving-lessons-learning-to-drive/practising-with-family-or-friends), last checked on 19 November 2026. This isn't insurance advice, and DriveSchoolPro doesn't sell or arrange insurance. Policies differ, so a pupil should always read the policy documents or ask the insurer.

## Why this is worth five minutes of a lesson

Private practice helps pupils keep what you teach them between lessons. GOV.UK is clear that there's no minimum number of lessons or practice hours, so how much a pupil does is up to them and their family. But two things can go badly wrong:

- **A pupil practises with no valid cover.** Often the family assumed a parent's policy covers "any driver", or that a provisional licence holder counts the same as a full one.
- **A pupil practises with the wrong supervisor.** An older sibling who passed two years ago doesn't meet the rules.

Either one can put points on a provisional licence before the pupil has taken a test. You aren't responsible for a family's insurance, but you are the person they trust on driving, and a short, accurate answer from you can stop it happening.

## What the law says about private practice

These are the rules GOV.UK gives for practising with family or friends.

| Rule | What GOV.UK says | If it's broken |
|---|---|---|
| Insurance | The learner must be insured to drive the car as a learner driver | An unlimited fine, a driving ban and up to 8 penalty points |
| Supervision | The supervisor must be over 21, qualified for that type of vehicle, and have held a full licence for 3 years | A fine of up to £1,000 and up to 6 penalty points on the provisional licence |
| L plates | An L plate on the front and back, easy to see (a D plate can be used in Wales) | Up to 6 penalty points |
| Motorways | A learner can't drive on a motorway when practising with family or friends | GOV.UK says it's illegal |
| Mobile phones | The friend or family member can't use a mobile phone while supervising | GOV.UK says it's illegal |

Three details are worth passing on:

1. **The supervisor's licence must match the car.** GOV.UK's example is that they need a manual licence to supervise in a manual car. The 3 years can be on a licence from the UK, the EU, Switzerland, Norway, Iceland or Liechtenstein.
2. **Motorways are for lessons with you.** A learner can drive on a motorway in England, Scotland or Wales only with an approved driving instructor in a car fitted with dual controls. See our guide to [motorway driving lessons](/blog/motorway-driving-lessons/).
3. **Only instructors can be paid.** Anyone a learner pays to teach them must be an ADI or a trainee instructor. A family friend can supervise for free but can't charge for it.

Learners can practise at any time of day or night. Our pupil-facing guide to [legal responsibilities when driving](/blog/legal-responsibilities-driving/) covers the licence, MOT and tax side.

## The three ways a learner can be insured

| Situation | How the pupil is covered | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| The pupil owns the car | Their own policy as a learner driver | That it covers a provisional licence holder, and who may supervise |
| A parent's or friend's car, on the owner's policy | The owner adds the pupil to their policy as a learner driver | The cost, any change to the excess, and what a claim would do to the owner's no claims bonus |
| A parent's or friend's car, on a separate policy | The pupil buys their own learner driver policy for that car | That the owner agrees, that the car is eligible, and how long the cover runs |

None of these is right for everyone. Being added to a parent's policy can be simple, but any claim is then made on that policy. A separate learner policy keeps the pupil's driving apart from the owner's insurance, and it can be bought for a short period, such as the weeks before a test. Collingwood, a specialist learner insurer, says on its site that an owner's no claims bonus "may be affected if a learner is added as an additional driver and has an accident". That's one reason families choose a separate policy.

The cheapest option depends on the pupil's age, the car and how long they'll practise for. Tell pupils to get more than one quote.

## Seven questions a pupil should ask the insurer

You can hand these over as they are.

1. **Am I covered as a provisional licence holder, in this car?** Each car normally needs its own policy.
2. **Who can supervise me?** The law says over 21 with a full licence for 3 years. GOV.UK notes that some insurers require the supervisor to be over 25.
3. **Does the supervisor need to be the car's owner, or insured on it?** Insurers differ.
4. **What level of cover is it?** Comprehensive, third party fire and theft, or third party only.
5. **What is the excess,** and if I'm on someone else's policy, what happens to their no claims bonus after a claim?
6. **Does it cover my driving test?**
7. **What happens when I pass?**

## Where your own insurance stops

Your instructor policy covers tuition in the car it insures. It doesn't cover a pupil driving their mum's car on a Sunday, and it doesn't cover you supervising in that car unless your insurer says so.

If a pupil asks you to teach in their own car:

- **Check both policies first.** Ask the pupil's insurer whether an instructor can supervise, and ask your own insurer whether you're covered to teach in a car that isn't yours.
- **Remember there are no dual controls.** That rules out motorways and changes how you manage risk in the lesson.
- **Check the car is legal.** It needs tax, a current MOT if it's over 3 years old, and L plates front and rear.

Some learner policies are written with this in mind. Collingwood's instructor page lists "teach students in their car", excluding motorways, among the features of its instructor programme. Read the policy wording before you rely on any insurer's summary.

## Test day in the pupil's own car

Some pupils want to take the test in the car they've practised in. GOV.UK says a car used for the driving test must:

- be insured for a driving test (the pupil should check with their insurance company)
- be taxed
- have a current MOT if it's over 3 years old
- have an extra interior rear-view mirror for the examiner
- show L plates on the front and rear (L or D plates in Wales)

It doesn't need dual controls. Some models can't be used at all because the examiner can't see all round, including panel vans and certain convertibles, and GOV.UK keeps the current list on its page about [using your own car for the test](https://www.gov.uk/driving-test/using-your-own-car).

Tell the pupil to ring the insurer well before the day and ask for confirmation in writing that the test is covered. GOV.UK says the test will be cancelled, and the pupil will have to pay again, if the car doesn't meet the rules.

## The day they pass

This is the gap most families miss. GOV.UK says that once you pass, you must have an insurance policy that allows you to drive without supervision. A learner policy is written for supervised driving.

So a pupil who passes at 10am shouldn't assume they can drive home alone on the policy they arrived with. Collingwood, for example, tells its customers that the supervising driver must drive them home after the test, pass or fail, and asks them to get in touch to cancel the policy once they've passed.

A simple line to give every pupil before their test: **"If you pass, phone your insurer before you drive anywhere on your own."**
